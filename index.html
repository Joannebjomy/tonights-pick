    Scene1

    Product & problem

    Netflix is great at recommending films to one person. Movie night is rarely one person.

    Every movie night with friends starts the same way: someone opens Netflix, everyone scrolls, nobody wants to be the one who picks, and half an hour later you're watching something only one person really wanted.

    Endless scrolling

    Recommendations are built for one profile. A group has to scroll and negotiate, film by film, out loud.

    The loudest voice wins

    People go along with the first confident suggestion. The quiet friend who hates horror sits through it anyway.

    No help choosing together

    Netflix recommends for one profile at a time, and watch-party tools sync playback, but nothing helps a group decide.

    Scene2

    My solution: Tonight's Pick

    A group mode for Netflix that turns "what should we watch?" into a two-minute vote.

    1. Start a movie night

      The host taps Movie night on the TV. Friends scan a QR code to join on their own phones. No account sharing needed.

    2. Set the ground rules

      How much time you have, the mood and the languages everyone understands. Netflix builds a shortlist of 8, skipping anything someone in the group has already seen.

    3. Vote in secret

      Everyone swipes on the same shortlist: Yes, Maybe or Not tonight. Nobody sees anyone else's votes until the end.

    4. Get a fair pick

      The app picks the film whose least happy person is happiest. Then it explains why, and the TV starts playing.

    The fairness rule

    Most voting apps pick the film with the most likes, even if one friend would hate it. Tonight's Pick uses a "least misery" rule from group-recommendation research instead:

    1. Prefer films where nobody said Not tonight.
    2. Among those, prefer the most Yes votes.
    3. Still tied? Pick the shorter film, because it's a school night.

    A film with three yes votes and one "not tonight" loses to a film with two yes and two maybe. Nobody spends the evening miserable.

    How I'd build it

    A live "session" connects the TV and everyone's phones. Votes travel through it in real time.

    Steps

    1. Sessions. The TV app creates a movie-night session with a short code and QR. Phones join it through a web page, so friends don't need the Netflix app.
    2. Shortlist. A service combines everyone's profiles: it drops anything someone has already watched, applies the time, mood and language filters, and ranks what's left.
    3. Live voting. Votes are sent over WebSockets and kept in Redis, hidden from other people until everyone's done.
    4. Fair pick. The least-misery rule picks the film and writes a one-line reason. The TV shows it and starts playing.
    5. Learn. After the film, a quick thumbs up or down helps the shortlist get better for that group next time.

    How I'd measure it

    • Time from opening Netflix to pressing play
    • How many movie nights end with a film playing
    • Thumbs up from everyone after the film
    Scene5

    Working prototype

    The streaming app at the top of this page works end to end, with a sample of 30 films: Marvel, Disney and Pixar, Malayalam favourites, and films in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Marathi.

    • Demo friends: with the box ticked, only you vote. Your friends' votes come from their tastes, for example Riya loves comedies and avoids sad films.
    • Pass the phone: untick the box and every guest votes in turn, with an "admit one" ticket screen in between so votes stay secret.
    • Try the fairness rule: say Not tonight to a film everyone else loves, and watch it lose.
    • Profile pictures: tap a guest's creature on their ticket to choose from nine movie buddies, from Moonshroom to Kernel the popcorn bucket.
    • Posters and creatures: drawn by the page itself as SVG, because real film posters are copyrighted.
    • Not built yet: separate phones joining live, and real Netflix data. Those are the backend steps in Scene 4.
